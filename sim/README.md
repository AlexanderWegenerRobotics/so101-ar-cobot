# sim

MuJoCo digital twin of the workspace: SO-101 arm on a table, two bins, AprilTag cubes and a RealSense D435i above the table.

## Files

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `model/` | SO-101 MJCF from MuJoCo Menagerie (`robotstudio_so101`), Apache 2.0 |
| `scene_builder.py` | Builds the scene from `config/scene.yaml` and writes the manifest |
| `tags.py` | Generates the AprilTag (tag36h11) and QR code textures |
| `hand.py` | Sim hand: scripted reaches into the workspace, or driven by the Quest hand stream |
| `world.py` | `SimWorld`: runs physics in real time in a background thread, rendering, ground truth poses |
| `generated/` | Output of the scene builder (gitignored) |

## Commands

```
python -m sim.scene_builder --view
python -m sim.scene_builder --seed 3 --render cam.png
mjpython -m sim.world
```

`--view` opens the scene in the MuJoCo viewer. `--render` saves one frame of the workspace camera. `sim.world` runs the live world with physics, the same thing the robot and camera APIs use.

## Scene

- World frame = robot base, z up, table top at z = 0.
- Base plate around the arm base, `model/assets/baseplate.stl`, generated by `hardware/baseplate/make_baseplate.py` (visual only, no collision). Its QR codes W1 to W3 come from `config/world_markers.yaml`, at the same positions as on the real plate. The Quest and the RealSense both calibrate to the robot frame from these codes. AprilTags are only used for bins and cubes.
- Bins: blue (tag 1) and red (tag 2), fixed positions. The tag sits on the bin floor. Perception only needs it once at startup, the bin pose is kept when cubes cover the tag later.
- Cubes: tag IDs from 10 upwards, tag on the top face, color on the sides and as a border around the tag. Random positions in the reachable area in front of the arm, repeatable through `seed`.
- Arm starts in the `home` keyframe (upright, out of the camera view), defined by `home_qpos` in `config/robot.yaml`.

## Cubes and heavy objects

`cubes` in `config/scene.yaml`:

- `count`: total number of cubes
- `color_ratio`: split between blue and red, turned into exact counts (8 at 50/50 gives 4 and 4)
- `types`: `standard` and `heavy`, each with `ratio`, `size` and `mass`

Heavy cubes look exactly like standard ones. Only their tag ID tells the robot that they are too heavy, so the operator cannot see it. In the sim they really weigh 1.5 kg, so the arm can't lift them in teleop either.

Around 16 cubes fit in the spawn area. If you ask for more, the builder stops with an error saying how many it placed.

## Manifest

Every build writes `generated/scene_manifest.json`. It lists for every tag ID the object name, type, color, size, mass and initial pose, plus the camera intrinsics. `task/objects.py` reads it to decide what the robot is allowed to pick. On the real setup this file gets written by hand once for the printed cubes.

## Camera

D435i color stream: 1920x1080, 30 fps, 42 degree vertical FOV (69 x 42), mounted 70 cm above the table looking straight down. A second camera `workspace_cam_depth` simulates the depth sensor (87 x 58 degree FOV, max 1280x720, about 1.5 cm offset from the color sensor). Depth and color are not aligned, same as on the real camera.

## Tag detection notes

In tests on rendered frames all tags are detected, with position errors around 1 to 2 mm. Two things matter for perception:

- Use corner refinement (`CORNER_REFINE_APRILTAG` in OpenCV). Without it the error is around 1 to 2 cm.
- Tilt estimates for the flat bin tags are noisy. Better to assume they lie flat on the table.

## Hand

Two hands (`left`, `right`) of 26 spheres each, in OpenXR joint order, the same joints Quest hand tracking gives. They are mocap bodies, so they move where they are told and don't collide by default (`hands.collide` in `scene.yaml`). They show up in the camera image, so perception sees realistic occlusion.

The `sim.hand:SimHand` node has two sources:

- `scripted`: the right hand rests on the human side of the table, reaches over one cube after another, holds, and pulls back (minimum jerk, timing in the node params). It publishes `hand` like the Quest would.
- `bus`: listens to `hand` from the Quest app and moves the sim hand to match.

## Camera calibration from the QR codes

`perception/qr_extrinsics.py` estimates the camera pose in the robot frame from the three plate codes. `python -m perception.qr_extrinsics --sim` checks it against the sim camera (about 1 mm and 0.1 degree on the current scene). Four things matter:

- Use `cv2.QRCodeDetectorAruco`. The plain `QRCodeDetector` misses the front code in the full frame.
- Use only the three finder pattern corners of each code. OpenCV guesses the fourth corner, it is off by 2 to 4 px.
- `cornerSubPix` with a small window (2 x 2). A 5 x 5 window pulls the corners off on the 4 px modules, the error goes up to about 1 cm.
- The front code W3 sits 22 mm in front of the base because the shoulder motor housing hides the strip right in front of the base from the camera.
